Advanced
Flight Seat Search

What the app does, and how to use it
A pocket-sized tool for people who pay for travel with points. It finds award seats across sixteen airline loyalty programs, tracks United upgrade space, and helps you decide whether points or cash is the better way to buy a given ticket.
User Guide · August 2026

1What the app is for

Six tools behind one passcode.

Award travel involves a lot of separate questions: is there a seat, on which airline, for how many points, and is that a good price? This app puts the answers in one place, on a screen sized for a phone.

Everything lives under six tabs across the top:

  • Region — browse award availability across whole parts of the world at once.
  • Route — check one specific airport pair over a range of dates.
  • UA Upgrades — find United flights with upgrade space open.
  • Alliances — look up which airlines belong where, and whose miles are interchangeable.
  • Points — decide whether to pay in points or cash for a specific ticket.
  • Transfers — see which credit card points move to which airline, and at what rate.

The app is built for a phone screen and works the same way in a desktop browser. A moon icon in the header switches the whole app between light and dark; the choice sticks for next time.

The ℹ️ button in the header opens a quick refresher on what each tab does.

Getting in

The app opens to a passcode pad. Tap in your four-digit PIN and it unlocks — there is no username to remember, and you stay unlocked for the rest of the browsing session.

  • Request Access — new users submit their name and email with the PIN they'd like, and get access once approved.
  • Forgot PIN — enter your email address and a temporary PIN is sent to you.
  • Change PIN — available any time from the header once you're inside.
The number in the header is your remaining search allowance for the day. Each availability search uses a little of it, and it resets daily. The Alliances, Points and Transfers tabs don't consume any of it.
The passcode screen, with access requests and PIN recovery underneath.

2Region — explore, then narrow

For when you know roughly where you'd like to go, but not exactly.

Region search answers the question "what can I get to Europe on, sometime in the next two months?" Instead of one airport pair, you pick a part of the world to leave from and a part of the world to land in, and the app sweeps everything in between.

You set four things before searching:

  • Mileage programs — tap as many of the sixteen as you want to search at once; at least one stays selected.
  • Cabin — Any, Economy, Premium, Business or First. Cabins are toggles, so you can look at business and first together.
  • Origin and destination regions — North and South America, Europe, Africa, Asia and Oceania. Choosing one opens a country list underneath, so you can narrow "Europe" down to just Portugal and Spain if you'd rather.
  • Date range — any window up to 60 days long.

Then tap Explore Region. Because a region sweep can take a while, a ✕ Stop button appears alongside the results if you want to cut it short and work with what has come back so far.

The Region tab: programs, cabin, both regions and dates on one screen.
Picking a region reveals its countries, so a broad search can be narrowed to specific destinations.
The same screen in dark mode — every tab follows the setting.

3Route — one city pair, many dates

For when the trip is already decided and you need a seat.

Route search takes an origin and a destination airport code and checks every date in your window across whichever programs you've selected. The program, cabin and date controls work exactly as they do on the Region tab.

The end date follows the start date automatically and is capped 60 days later, so you can't accidentally ask for a year at a time.

Tap Search Availability and the results appear underneath — one card per bookable itinerary, sorted by date, with a running count in the corner.

Both searches share the same results area, the same filters and the same calendar view, so anything you learn on one tab carries straight over to the other.

4Reading and working with results

What each card tells you, and the four ways to cut the list down.

Every result card carries the essentials at a glance:

  • The airports and their city names, and the date of travel.
  • Which loyalty program the seat is priced in — the badge on the right.
  • The cabin, how many seats are left at that price, and whether the itinerary is nonstop or how many stops it has.
  • Departure and arrival times, the flight numbers, and a +1 marker when you land the following day.
  • The points price and any taxes and surcharges due on top.

▼ Details expands the card to show the aircraft, the operating carrier and, on connecting itineraries, the connection breakdown. Book → opens the airline's own booking page with the route and date already filled in, and the 📤 button hands you a tidy summary of the find to send to someone.

Results cap out at the count shown next to the heading; ⬇ CSV exports what's currently on screen to a spreadsheet.

Results for one route: cabin, seats left, routing, times and points on every card.
Details expanded — aircraft, carrier and timings for the itinerary.
Filters sit above the list: sort order, program, airport, airline, and nonstop-only.

Narrowing a long list

A wide search can return hundreds of cards. The controls above the results let you sort by date, points, journey length or program, and filter down to a single program, a particular connecting airport, a specific airline, or nonstop flights only. Clear all filters puts everything back.

The calendar view

📅 Calendar swaps the list for a month-by-month grid showing the cheapest price available on each day. Days are color-coded — green for the cheapest dates in your results, amber for middling, red for the priciest — so a good week jumps out immediately.

Tap any day to filter the results down to that date, and ☰ List to switch back. It's the fastest way to answer "when is this trip cheapest?" without reading a single card.

5UA Upgrades — where the upgrade space is

United flights with confirmable upgrade inventory open.

This tab tracks upgrade availability on United flights, so you can point your PlusPoints at a flight that will actually clear rather than sitting on a waitlist.

You can narrow the list by:

  • Origin and destination airport, or a whole region on either end.
  • A date range.
  • A specific flight number, or an aircraft type such as "777".
  • A minimum amount of upgrade inventory — anything from one seat up to nine.

Sort by date, by how much inventory is open, or by airport, then tap Search Upgrades.

Each card shows the route and cities, the date and flight number, the aircraft, the scheduled times where they're known, and how much upgrade space is open — colored green when there's plenty and amber when it's thinner. Underneath is how recently that flight was last confirmed to have space.

Upgrade filters, with a note showing how current the list is.

Every card has a Book on United → link and a Share button, and the whole filtered list can be exported with ⬇ CSV. Long lists show the first 200 matches, so tighten the filters if you hit that.

Above the results, 📡 Request Updated Data asks for a fresh sweep if the list is looking stale — useful when you're planning around a date that's close.

Upgrade results out of Chicago, sorted by date, showing open inventory per flight.

6Alliances — who flies with whom

A reference for the question "can I book this airline with those miles?"

The Alliances tab is a directory of Star Alliance, oneworld and SkyTeam, listing every member airline together with the loyalty program its miles actually live in.

The Shared Mileage Programs panel at the top is the part worth knowing about. Several airlines pool into a single currency — Lufthansa, Swiss, Austrian, Brussels, LOT and Croatia all sit in Miles & More, and British Airways, Finnair, Iberia and Qatar all sit in Avios. Miles earned on any of them are spendable across the rest, and the app flags those relationships on each airline as you scroll.

The search box at the top matches on airline name, two-letter code or program name, so typing "Avios", "AY" or "Finnair" all land in the same place. Membership reflects recent moves between alliances rather than the line-up from a few years ago.

Shared currencies first, then each alliance with its members and programs.

Searching for a single carrier collapses everything down to the match, showing its alliance and which other programs share its currency — a two-second answer to "where do I need miles to fly this airline?"

One airline, its alliance, and the currency it shares with three others.

7Points — is this worth paying in miles?

A straight verdict on points versus cash for one specific ticket.

You've found an award seat. Should you actually use your points on it? This tab settles that. Pick the airline, then enter three numbers:

Cash priceWhat the same ticket would cost you in money.
Points requiredThe award price the airline is quoting.
Taxes & feesThe cash still due on the award ticket.

The app works out what each point is buying you, after subtracting the taxes you'd pay anyway, and compares it against what points in that particular program are generally reckoned to be worth. The verdict comes back as one of three answers:

  • Use Points — you're getting meaningfully more than the going rate.
  • Toss-Up — close enough to the baseline that other factors should decide.
  • Pay Cash — you'd be spending points cheaply; save them for a better trip.

Underneath is the full arithmetic, so you can see how the answer was reached rather than taking it on faith. Your last entry is remembered when you come back to the tab.

The baselines are rough consensus estimates of what a point is worth, not promises. Taxes and carrier surcharges vary a lot by route and program.
A verdict with the full breakdown underneath.

8Transfers — moving card points to airlines

Which points go where, at what rate, and what's on sale right now.

Most award bookings start with credit card points rather than airline miles. This tab lays out the transfer partners for the six major rewards currencies — American Express, Chase, Capital One, Citi, Bilt and Wells Fargo — with the ratio for each partner airline and a badge showing which alliance that airline belongs to.

Two views: By Card groups partners under each rewards program, which is what you want when you're deciding where your existing points can go. By Airline flips it around to show which cards feed a given airline — the view for "I need Turkish miles, who can get me there?" The search box filters either view by airline, code or program name.

When a transfer bonus is running, it appears inline: a banner counts how many are live, and the affected partners show the boosted ratio with the standard one struck through, along with the bonus percentage and its expiry. Bonuses appear for everyone as soon as they're posted.

Worth repeating: transfers are one-way and can't be reversed. Confirm the ratio in your own account before moving anything — published rates change often, and some cards in a family transfer at a lower rate than others.

9Small things that help

Habits worth picking up.
  • Search wide, then filter. Selecting several programs at once and narrowing afterwards costs the same as one narrow search, and you see more.
  • Check the calendar before reading cards. Shifting a trip by two days is often the cheapest change you can make.
  • Use Share rather than screenshots. The summary it produces carries the route, cabin, price, taxes and a booking link in one message.
  • Export before the list changes. Award space moves; a CSV is a snapshot you can compare against later.
  • Stop a long search early. Region sweeps keep whatever they've already found when you tap ✕ Stop.
  • Check the Points tab before transferring. It costs nothing to run the numbers, and a transfer can't be undone.
  • Dark mode. The moon icon in the header, on the passcode screen too.

Availability shown here is what the airlines were offering when the search ran. Seats come and go quickly — always confirm on the airline's own site before making plans around a find.
