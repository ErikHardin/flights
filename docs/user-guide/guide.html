Flight Seat Search

What the app does, and how to use it
A pocket-sized tool for people who pay for travel with points. It finds award seats across sixteen airline loyalty programs, tracks United upgrade space, and helps you decide whether points or cash is the better way to buy a given ticket.

1What the app is for

Six tools behind one passcode.

Award travel involves a lot of separate questions: is there a seat, on which airline, for how many points, and is that a good price? This app puts the answers in one place, on a screen sized for a phone — and works the same way in a desktop browser.

2Region — explore, then narrow

For when you know roughly where you'd like to go, but not exactly.

Region search answers the question "what can I get to Europe on, sometime in the next two months?" Instead of one airport pair, you pick a part of the world to leave from and a part of the world to land in, and the app sweeps everything in between. Then tap Explore Region — and because a sweep can take a while, a ✕ Stop button appears alongside the results if you want to cut it short and work with what has come back so far.

3Route — one city pair, many dates

For when the trip is already decided and you need a seat.

Route search takes an origin and a destination airport code and checks every date in your window across whichever programs you've selected. The program, cabin and date controls work exactly as they do on the Region tab, and the end date follows the start date automatically, capped 60 days later.

Tap Search Availability and the results appear underneath — one card per bookable itinerary, sorted by date, with a running count in the corner.

Reach for Region when…Reach for Route when…
You know the continent, not the cityBoth airports are already fixed
You're hunting for any good redemptionYou need this particular trip
The dates are flexible and wideYou want every date for one pair
Both searches share the same results area, the same filters and the same calendar view, so anything you learn on one tab carries straight over to the other.

6UA Upgrades — where the upgrade space is

United flights with confirmable upgrade inventory open.

This tab tracks upgrade availability on United flights, so you can point your PlusPoints at a flight that will actually clear rather than sitting on a waitlist. Set the filters, then tap Search Upgrades. The whole filtered list exports with ⬇ CSV, long lists show the first 200 matches, and 📡 Request Updated Data asks for a fresh sweep when the list looks stale.

7Alliances — who flies with whom

A reference for the question "can I book this airline with those miles?"

The Alliances tab is a directory of the three global alliances, listing every member airline together with the loyalty program its miles actually live in. The search box matches on airline name, two-letter code or program name, so "Avios", "AY" and "Finnair" all land in the same place.

8Points — is this worth paying in miles?

A straight verdict on points versus cash for one specific ticket.

You've found an award seat. Should you actually use your points on it? Pick the airline, then enter three numbers:

InputWhat to enter
Cash priceWhat the same ticket would cost you in money
Points requiredThe award price the airline is quoting
Taxes & feesThe cash still due on the award ticket

The app works out what each point is buying you, after subtracting the taxes you'd pay anyway, and compares that against what points in that program are generally reckoned to be worth. Underneath the verdict is the full arithmetic, so you can see how the answer was reached rather than taking it on faith. Your last entry is remembered.

A verdict with the full breakdown underneath.
VerdictWhen you'll see itWhat to do
Use PointsEach point buys meaningfully more than the program's baselineBook the award
Toss-UpClose to the baselineLet other factors decide
Pay CashBelow the baseline, or the taxes swallow the fareSave the points for a better trip
The baselines are rough consensus estimates of what a point is worth, not promises. Taxes and carrier surcharges vary a lot by route and program.

9Transfers — moving card points to airlines

Which points go where, at what rate, and what's on sale right now.

Most award bookings start with credit card points rather than airline miles. This tab lays out the transfer partners for the six major rewards currencies, with the ratio for each partner airline and a badge showing which alliance that airline belongs to.

When a transfer bonus is running it appears inline: a banner counts how many are live, and the affected partners show the boosted ratio with the standard one struck through, along with the bonus percentage and its expiry. Bonuses appear for everyone as soon as they're posted.

Worth repeating: transfers are one-way and can't be reversed. Confirm the ratio in your own account before moving anything — published rates change often, and some cards in a family transfer at a lower rate than others.
